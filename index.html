What is a cemí? To the Taíno people of the Caribbean, a cemí was a deity or ancestral spirit — and the carved, three-pointed stone or wooden figure that housed it. Cemís were consulted for healing and guidance, fed with offerings, and kept as a living link between a community and its ancestors. In this book, that link is very much alive in the modern day.

Synopsis

At twenty-five, JC is an artist in the Bronx, racing to finish a mural project built on augmented reality while the rent keeps stacking up. Then his equipment starts picking up an eight-second audio loop that dulls both sound and emotion — and a coin he inherited begins to radiate warmth in his palm, its pattern resurfacing again and again.

The loop pulls JC into a family legacy intertwined with Taíno ancestral echoes, and into the orbit of Aperture, an organization that has long been converting human feelings into a commodity. Aperture offers real community support — but JC soon learns there are hidden costs, and they are being paid by the people he loves.

Alongside his friend Manny, a conero, and the people who raised him, JC journeys through the Bronx and Santo Domingo to uncover the truth. His undiagnosed ADHD complicates his work, his relationships, and his ability to trust his own perceptions. Now he must figure out how to interpret his experiences, discern whom to rely on, and learn to ask for help — before Aperture's influence tightens its grip on his community.

About the Author

Luis M. Minier is a writer and independent software creator based in New York City. Heralds of the Cemí is his debut novel — a story of Dominican heritage, Taíno ancestral memory, and Bronx resilience, told through the eyes of an artist whose greatest instrument is the way he can't help but see the world.
